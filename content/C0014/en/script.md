## Narration

1. Is marriage the same in every faith?
2. For Hindus, vivaha is a samskara, a rite of passage, traditionally sealed by seven steps around the fire.
3. In Islam, the nikah is a contract and a solemn pledge, with a gift for the bride called mahr.
4. And the Qur'an speaks of tranquillity, and of love and mercy between the two.
5. For Christians, two become one flesh, and Catholics and Orthodox call it a sacrament.
6. Jews call it kiddushin, sanctification, and wed beneath a canopy that stands for the new home.
7. Sikhs call it Anand Karaj, the ceremony of bliss, as the couple circle the Guru Granth Sahib four times.
8. Many Buddhists see it as a social bond, and the Buddha taught spouses to be faithful.
9. So no, not the same, yet each asks two people to keep a promise and build a home.

## Accuracy and sensitivity notes

- Each tradition is described in its own terms and with its own word; no ranking or comparison words. Every tradition gets one scene; Islam gets two so that 'contract' is immediately followed by the Qur'an's words on tranquillity, love and mercy.
- Diversity inside traditions: the sacrament is attributed only to Catholic and Orthodox teaching (many Protestant churches do not count marriage as a sacrament, CoE Art. XXV); 'many Buddhists' (practice differs by country); Hindu practice of seven steps vs seven circuits varies (the script uses the term's meaning).
- Cut: 'both must consent' for the nikah (schools differ); the number of saṃskāras, pheras and per-step vows; Jainism and Zoroastrianism (omitted honestly, not researched to standard in this pass).
- Depiction: no God, prophet or Guru as a figure. The Guru Granth Sahib appears only as a covered book on a palki; the Qur'an is not shown; a small plain cross on a stand marks the Christian scene. Brides' heads are covered where customary.
- No politics, no modern controversies (interfaith or legal disputes are not mentioned).
- Source caveat: Britannica, BBC Religions and the Oxford Dictionary of World Religions could not be fetched (403 / blocked / paywalled); the script relies on scripture in named translations, official documents of the traditions, a statute and Wikipedia with its cited sources. A Britannica cross-check before publishing is recommended.
- Sikh scene: the couple are barefoot before the Guru Granth Sahib (Sikh Rehat Maryada Art. V(j): shoes are taken off in the gurdwara). Hindi narration says they circle (परिक्रमा) the Guru Granth Sahib; the rite is never called फेरे.
