- Series: Chalk Tales · Puppet Explainers (sample episode) · format: 9:16 Short · languages: en, hi
- Researched 2026-11-24 by the scriptwriter. Every URL below was fetched on **2026-11-24** unless marked otherwise.
- Principle (comparative-religion version of the storytelling accuracy rules): each tradition is described in its own terms, with its own word for marriage; scripture is quoted only from a named translation; diversity inside a tradition is stated where a claim does not hold for all ("Catholics and Orthodox", "many Buddhists"); no ranking, no "true/best" language, no politics or modern controversies.
- Source types: **Text** (scripture), **Teaching / code** (an official body of the tradition), **Reference** (encyclopaedia or scholarship), **Practice** (how the rite is usually performed; varies by region and community).

## The closing line
"So no, not the same, yet each asks two people to keep a promise and build a home" / "तो मतलब अलग हैं, पर हर परंपरा वचन निभाने और घर बसाने को कहती है". Support: a binding promise or obligation in each (rows 2, 3–4, 7, 10–11, 14, 17) and the home or household as its setting (rows 1, 7 "partnership of the whole of life", 12, 17). The line first says the meanings differ, and it does not call every marriage sacred, because Buddhism (row 16) explicitly does not.

## Cut or not used
- "Both must consent" for Islam: cut (schools differ, row 3).
- "Covenant" as the word for all Christians: dropped; the shared scripture line is used, and the sacrament is named only for Catholics and Orthodox (rows 6–9).
- The number of saṃskāras, the number of pheras and the per-step vows: not stated (they vary).
- Buddhist poruwa platform and Sri Lankan dress: visual direction only, as one cultural form; no narrated claim.
- Jainism and Zoroastrianism: omitted. Not researched to this standard in this pass, and six traditions already fill a ~50 s Short. Candidates for a follow-up.
- Britannica, BBC Religions and the Oxford Dictionary of World Religions could not be used (Britannica 403, BBC unreachable for the fetch tool, ODWR paywalled). Rows use scripture in named translations, official documents (Vatican Catechism, Sikh Rehat Maryada, Church of England Articles), a statute, bodies of the traditions (HAF, Chabad, My Jewish Learning), a Buddhist teacher's book and, where noted, Wikipedia with its cited sources. A Britannica cross-check before publishing is recommended.

## Depiction rules applied
- No God, prophet or Guru as a figure. The Guru Granth Sahib appears only as a covered book on a palki under a small canopy; the Qur'an is not shown (that scene shows the couple only); no image of Jesus or the Buddha. A small plain cross on a stand marks the Christian scene.
- Traditional, modest attire for each tradition; heads covered where customary. No ranking or comparison words.
- Each tradition has one scene of similar length; Islam has two (contract, then the Qur'an's own words on tranquillity and mercy) so that "contract" is never left without the scripture's framing.
