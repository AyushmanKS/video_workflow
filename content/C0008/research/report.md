# Research report: C0008 (scriptwriter)

**Topic:** the road to Mithila. The departure from Siddhashrama and the halt by the Shona (Bala Kanda 1.31.6–24), and the story Vishvamitra tells that night about King Kushanabha's hundred daughters (1.32.11–1.33.23). The teaser comes from sarga 34. **Continuity:** Episode 07 ended on the teaser verse 1.31.9 ("no god, gandharva, asura or rakshasa can string it, still less men") and the card "Next: Janaka's Bow".

## Reference corrections and notes
- **sa.wikisource prints 1.31.19 twice.** One copy is broken and one is the full couplet. valmikiramayan.net uses half-verse offsets (19b–20a … 22b–23a). The scripts cite ranges (1.31.19–23) that agree in both.
- **The verse card is the second half of 1.33.8 and the first half of 1.33.9** (valmikiramayan.net labels it "8b, 9a"), so it is cited 1.33.8–9. Reading: यज्ञाश्च on sa.wikisource, यज्ञश्च on valmikiramayan.net; the card follows sa.wikisource.
- **Other variant readings, not quoted:** 1.32.7 असूर्तरजसो / अधूर्तरजसो; 1.32.17 अक्षयं / अक्षय्यं; 1.32.20 सर्वाः समस्ताः / देवं समस्ता. sa.wikisource's 1.32.24 adds अरत्निमात्राकृतयो… ("reduced to a cubit"), which valmikiramayan.net lacks, so it is not used.
- **Kanyakubja** (the Kannauj etymology) does not appear in the vulgate text of sargas 32–33 on either site, so it is not used. Kushanabha's city is Mahodaya (1.32.6).
- The scripts don't place the daughters at the Shona camp, because 1.32.8–10 calls that land Vasu's.
- **"Forgiveness"** is the father's word (1.33.6), and the scripts attribute it to him. **"Grandfather"** joins 1.34.5 and 1.34.6.
- **Lakshmana** is present (1.31.21). He is named in te (రామలక్ష్మణులతో); the other languages say "the princes".
- **Depiction:** Vayu is symbolic (a wind spiral), per the character bible rule for deities. The princesses are modest and distant. The curse is shown as bending like reeds in the wind, with no deformity.
