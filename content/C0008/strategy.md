# Strategy: C0008 (scriptwriter)

**Decision:** continue in narrative order from 1.31.9. Pay off the Ep07 teaser in the opening (scene 2: the bow no king could string, 1.31.10), then tell the one self-contained story of the road's first night: King Kushanabha's hundred daughters, who refused the wind god (1.32.11–1.33.23). Format: 35–55 s, hard limit under 60 s. Lint estimates: en 47.5 s, hi 46.8 s, gu 47.3 s, te 49.1 s, mr 49.6 s. gu/te/mr stay ≤50 s as the brief asks.

## Why this beat
The brief offered three candidates.
- **Departure (1.31.8–19): rejected as the main beat.** It has the bow's origin and the birds and deer following, but no conflict or turn. It is used as context in scene 2, paying off the teaser.
- **Shona halt and Vishvamitra's lineage (1.31.19–1.34.13): chosen.** Inside the "lineage" is the only complete dramatic arc in sargas 31–34:
  - **Stakes:** a god offers immortality (1.32.16–17).
  - **Refusal:** the daughters laugh; they could unseat him but guard their tapas, and only their father gives them in marriage (1.32.18–22).
  - **Turn:** in rage he bends them (1.32.23–26).
  - **Reversal:** their father does not rage but praises their forgiveness, "on forgiveness the world stands" (1.33.6–9).
  - **Payoff:** a king's touch makes them straight again (1.33.23).
  - **Reveal:** that king Kushanabha was the sage's own grandfather (1.34.5–6).
  This fits 35–55 s without inventing anything, and it gives the series its first named moral verse since the Manava card.
- **Ganga at dawn (sarga 35): deferred.** It comes after sarga 34 in narrative order, so jumping there would skip the lineage. As a 45 s episode it would also be mostly travel plus a question, with the answer (the Ganga's descent) belonging to later episodes.

## Depiction (direction for the creative director, who writes the prompts)
- Rama is always seen from behind.
- Vayu is only a chalk wind spiral, with no face or body (deities symbolic).
- The hundred princesses are a modest, distant group, never sexualised (they are described as beautiful in 1.32.12–14; the visuals stay restrained).
- The curse is bodies bending like windblown reeds with hands over faces, with no deformity close-ups.
- Brahmadatta's touch is shown as a hand-taking in a wedding hall with golden light.
- Architecture is ancient Indian.
- Use the series tokens {rama_teen}, {lakshmana_teen} and {vishvamitra}. New figures (Kushanabha, Brahmadatta, the princesses) have no character tokens yet. The creative director may add them to `profiles/characters/ramayana.json`.
