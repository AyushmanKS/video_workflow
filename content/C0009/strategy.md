# Strategy: C0009 (scriptwriter)

**Decision:** continue in strict narrative order from 1.34.8. Pay off the Ep08 teaser in the first line. The Kaushiki beat (1.34.7–12) is too thin on its own for 45–53 s, so the episode joins all three candidates the way the text tells them, as one night and one morning:
- the sister river and the sage's love and vow (1.34.7–12);
- the hushed midnight (1.34.14–17, 23);
- the dawn march to the Ganga, where Rama asks how she crosses three worlds (1.35.1–12).

The episode ends on that question (teaser 1.35.12), with the card "Next: Ganga's Birth" (1.35.13). It does not jump ahead to Ahalya or the bow.

Lint estimates are en 52.89 s, hi 52.63 s, gu 52.79 s, te 52.98 s and mr 52.76 s. All are inside the owner's 45–53 s flow band, with te and mr at or under 53 s.

## Why this shape
- **Kaushiki alone: rejected.** The beat runs from wonder (1.34.8) to love (1.34.10) to vow (1.34.12) in about 25 s of telling. Padding it would mean adding motive or later tradition (the Richika–Jamadagni story), which the rules forbid.
- **Night alone: rejected.** It is beautiful but has no turn.
- **Combined: chosen.** Each part grows out of the one before: the story is told, the hour grows late, the sage bids sleep, dawn comes, and the next river raises the next question. This is a natural kathavachak telling: one night by one river, then morning at another. The rivers rhyme: the sister who became a river, and then the Ganga, whose birth (as Himavan's elder daughter, 1.35.16) is the next episode.
