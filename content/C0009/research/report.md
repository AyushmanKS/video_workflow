# Research report: C0009 (scriptwriter)

**Topic:** the sister river. On the night at the Shona camp (Bala Kanda 1.34.7–23), Vishvamitra tells Rama about his elder sister Satyavati, who followed her husband Richika to heaven in her own body and flowed forth as the Kaushiki. The sage lives beside her on Himavat out of love, and left her only for his vow, which Rama's splendour fulfilled. He bids Rama sleep at midnight and describes the hushed night. At dawn they march on and reach the Ganga, where Rama asks how she crosses three worlds to the sea (1.35.1–12).

**Continuity:** Episode 08 ended on the teaser "his elder sister became a river" (VR 1.34.7–8) and the card "Next: The Sister River". Scene 1 pays this off.

**Edition:** vulgate (southern recension).

## Reference corrections and notes
- **Variant readings, not quoted.**
  - 1.34.3 has पुत्रस्ते सदृशः पुत्र (sa.wikisource) and पुत्र ते सदृशः पुत्रो (valmikiramayan.net).
  - 1.34.17 has उत्तिष्ठते and उत्तिष्ठति.
  - 1.35.3 has कृतपूर्वाह्णिकक्रियः and कृत्वा पौर्वाह्णिकक्रियः.
  - 1.35.6 has विश्वामित्रेण and विश्वमित्रेण.
  - None of these affects the scripts.
- **The verse card (1.34.10) is identical on both sites.** The card follows sa.wikisource's sandhi (रघुनन्दन; valmikiramayan.net prints रघुनंदन).
- **"In her own body"** renders सशरीरा (1.34.8). The scripts do not say "alive" or "without dying". valmikiramayan.net glosses "her departed husband", but the verse says only "following her husband", so the scripts do not say he had died.
- **"For the good of the world"** renders लोकस्य हितकार्यार्थं (1.34.9); a variant reads हितकामार्थं, with the same sense.
- **"True to her husband"** draws on भर्तारमनुवर्तिनी (1.34.8) and पतिव्रता (1.34.11).
- **"By Rama's splendour that vow was fulfilled"** condenses सिद्धाश्रममनुप्राप्तः सिद्धोऽस्मि तव तेजसा (1.34.12), literally "I have succeeded by your splendour". It refers to the protected sacrifice of Ep06–07 and is attributed to the sage.
- **The hushed night (scene 8)** is the sage's own description in 1.34.15–17, told as scene narration with nothing added. The night creatures (1.34.18) appear only in the visual direction.
- **en scene 7** reports 1.34.14 (गतोऽर्धरात्रः; मा भूद् विघ्नोऽध्वनीह नः). The other languages give it after a soft speech lead-in.
- **"By midday"** renders गतेऽर्धदिवसे (1.35.7). "At dawn they set out" matches 1.35.3 and 1.35.6.
- **"Crossed three worlds to reach the sea"** renders त्रैलोक्यं कथमाक्रम्य गता नदनदीपतिम् (1.35.12). नदनदीपति, "lord of rivers", is the ocean.
- **Lakshmana** is present: सहसौमित्रिः (1.34.23) and सहराघवाः (1.35.8). All languages name him in scene 9.
- **The rivers are shown as rivers.** Neither Satyavati nor the Ganga is drawn as a woman; scene 3 is a symbolic path of light.

## Later traditions
None are used.
- The Kosi identification (a Gorresio note quoted on valmikiramayan.net) is excluded, and the scripts never say the river "still flows today".
- The Richika–Satyavati–Jamadagni story (Mahabharata, Puranas) is not in these sargas and is not used.
- Ramcharitmanas, Geet Ramayan, Molla and Giridhar were not consulted for this beat.
