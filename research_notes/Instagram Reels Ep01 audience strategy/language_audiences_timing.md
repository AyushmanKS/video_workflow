# Instagram audiences and posting times for the en / hi / gu / te Ramayana chalk Reels (Ep01, Nov 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-16. Free web sources only. Each number carries its source. Data older than 2024 is marked **[pre-2024]**. Where sources disagree, the notes say so. No figure here comes from @mr.aks.tale's own Insights. The account is new and has no audience data yet.

## Q1. Instagram's user base in India: size, age and gender, language preferences, regional-language growth

### Takeaway
India is Instagram's largest market. The ad audience was about 481M in late 2025, up 22.9% in a year. It skews heavily male (about 70%) and young. Meta is investing openly in Indic-language creation tools: AI Reel translation now covers Hindi and Telugu, but not Gujarati.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram ad reach in India was **481 million** in late 2025. That is 32.8% of the total population, 43.9% of adults 18+ and 46.8% of internet users. It grew by **+89.5M (+22.9%)** from Oct 2024 to Oct 2025, and by +7.95M (+1.7%) from Jul to Oct 2025. DataReportal stresses that these are ad-audience estimates, not monthly active users. — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-india)
- Gender split of the adult Instagram ad audience in India: **29.9% female, 69.7% male** (late 2025). — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-india)
- India context: 1.03B internet users (70.0% penetration, Oct 2025) and 500M social media user identities. Median age is 28.8. People aged 18–34 make up 29.1% of the population (18–24: 12.3%, 25–34: 16.8%). — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-india)
- An independent panel measure differs from the ad-reach figure. Comscore's *State of Social India 2025* puts Instagram at **337 million unique visitors (June 2025)** and says Reels make up **69% of all actions on Instagram** in 2025, up from 19% five years earlier. I saw this only in secondary coverage and did not read the Comscore report itself. — [Comscore figure via Indiantelevision / afaqs coverage](https://www.afaqs.com/news/digital/five-years-of-reels-how-instagrams-algorithm-became-indias-tastemaker-10464781)
- The secondary figures conflict. Aggregators quote 414M users (DataReportal, Jan 2025), 481M and 362M (Statista). Use DataReportal 481M as an ad-reach figure, not a user count. — [search summary of SocialPilot / Statista / DataReportal pages](https://datareportal.com/essential-instagram-stats)
- Instagram took a **65% share of digital ad impressions** in India in 2025 (TAM AdEx). — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/instagram-accounts-for-65-of-indias-digital-ad-impressions-in-2025-tam-adex-152603.html)
- Meta announced at "House of Instagram" in Mumbai (28 Nov 2025, updated 15–16 Jan 2026) that Meta AI Reel translation with optional lip sync now covers **Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Marathi**, alongside Hindi, English, Spanish and Portuguese. The Indian-language rollout began for everyone on 16 Jan 2026. Edits added **Devanagari** (Hindi, Marathi) and **Bengali-Assamese** fonts. **Gujarati is not in either list.** — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/11/instagram-empowers-creators-to-go-global-with-local-voice-translations-and-fonts/)
- Meta India's head (company-authored op-ed) wrote that **98% of internet users access content in Indic languages** and **~57% of urban internet users prefer regional-language content**. The piece names Tamil, Telugu and Malayalam as the most popular. These are Meta's own claims, and the underlying study is not named in the summary I saw. — [Outlook Business, Sandhya Devanathan](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/magazine/sandhya-devanathan-writes-rise-of-regional-creators-will-drive-next-gen-creativity)
- A Meta-commissioned study reports that **92% of Indian users prefer Reels** over other short-video formats. This is self-commissioned. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/92-indians-prefer-instagram-reels-over-youtube-shorts-report)

### Inferences
- The default viewer in India is a man aged 18–34. Captions should not assume an older, family-elder viewer, even for devotional content.
- Viewers who don't speak Hindi or Telugu can watch those versions through Meta AI translation. Gujarati has no such tool, so the gu version relies on its native script and Gujarati hashtags for discovery.
- Instagram has no published figure for its user split by language. The language mix has to be inferred from Census speaker counts (Q2).

### Gaps
- No primary Instagram figure for users by language or by state.
- The Comscore and Meta op-ed figures came through secondary coverage. I did not read either primary report.
- I found no free KPMG/EY 2025 media report section on regional-language short video in the time I had.

## Q2. Speaker populations, Indian home states and diaspora hubs, with time zones

### Takeaway
Hindi is the largest audience by far (52.8 crore first-language speakers, 2011). Telugu (8.1 crore) and Gujarati (5.5 crore) are much smaller in India but have strong US diasporas. Gujarati is also large in the UK, Canada and East Africa. Telugu is the fastest-growing Indian language in the US.

### Cited Findings
- **Census of India 2011** [pre-2024; no newer census language data has been published]. First-language speakers: **Hindi 528,347,193 (41.1%)**, **Telugu 81,127,740**, **Gujarati 55,492,554**. One expert suggests the Gujarati figure may be inflated. — [Census 2011 tables (ORGI)](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/10220/study-description); figures as summarised by [Business Standard/PTI](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/10k-more-sanskrit-speakers-in-india-in-2011-census-118071500152_1.html) and [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/hindi-fastest-growing-language-between-2001-2011)
- About **139 million** more Indians speak Hindi as a second language (Census 2011) [pre-2024]. — [search summary of Census coverage](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/List_of_Indian_languages_by_number_of_native_speakers)
- Home states (general knowledge, consistent with Census 2011): Hindi in the Hindi belt (UP, Bihar, MP, Rajasthan, Delhi, Haryana, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, HP). Gujarati in Gujarat plus Mumbai. Telugu in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Hyderabad. All of India uses IST (UTC+5:30).
- **US**: The Center for Immigration Studies (2018, using 2010–2017 ACS) found native Telugu speakers grew **86%**, the largest growth of any foreign-language group. Hindi remained the largest Indian language [pre-2024]. — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/news/telugu-fastest-growing-language-us-says-study-90332)
- **US**: A 2024 report citing Census data via Statistical Atlas says Telugu speakers rose from **~320,000 (2016) to ~1.23 million (2024)**. It ranks Telugu as the third Indian language in the US after Hindi and Gujarati. **Conflict:** a language map citing ACS 2023 lists Telugu at 91,144 and Hindi at 135,750, which looks like a sub-national or sample figure. Neither number was verified against ACS table B16001. — [Perigon news summary](https://perigon.io/news/world/2024/06/26/teluguspeaking-population-us-surges-fourfold); [Jeenie language map](https://jeenie.com/hubfs/Geographic%20Language%20Map-v5.25.3.pdf)
- **US (Pew, 2021–23 ACS)**: Among Asian Americans aged 5+, **Hindi 4%** and **Telugu 2%** speak the language at home. Pew gives no absolute counts. — [Pew Research chart](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/10-most-spoken-languages-among-the-asian-population-2023/)
- **Canada (2021 Census)**: Speakers of non-official languages include **Hindi 761,000** and **Gujarati 209,000**. Hindi grew 66% and Gujarati 43% from 2016 to 2021. A narrower measure, the language spoken *predominantly* at home, gives Gujarati 92,000. — [Statistics Canada, 2025 release](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-627-m/11-627-m2025004-eng.htm); [CIC News](https://cicnews.com/2022/08/statistics-canada-report-states-language-diversity-due-to-immigration-in-canada-0830134.html)
- **UK (England & Wales, Census 2021)**: Gujarati is the **main language of about 189,000 people (0.3%)** and is among the top 10 non-English main languages. I saw this in ICAEW's summary of the ONS release of 29 Nov 2022, not the ONS table TS024 itself. — [ICAEW](https://icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2022/dec-2022/Chart-of-the-week-Language-proficiency-in-England-and-Wales); [ONS TS024](https://www.ons.gov.uk/datasets/TS024)
- **Time zones in Nov–Dec 2026.** US DST ends Sun 1 Nov 2026 and the UK changed on 25 Oct 2026, so both are on standard time. IST is UTC+5:30. Offsets from IST:
  - US Eastern (EST, UTC−5) is IST −10:30.
  - US Pacific (PST, UTC−8) is IST −13:30.
  - UK (GMT) is IST −5:30.
  - Gulf (UAE, UTC+4) is IST −1:30.
  - East Africa (EAT, UTC+3) is IST −2:30.
  - Sydney (AEDT, UTC+11) is IST +5:30.
  (Standard time-zone facts; no single source cited.)

### Inferences
- **Hindi version**: mostly a domestic audience across north and central India. Gulf workers (IST −1:30) watch at almost the same clock time.
- **Gujarati version**: Gujarat and Mumbai, plus a large share of diaspora in the UK, US (NJ, Texas, California), Canada and East Africa. The UK and East Africa are only 2:30–5:30 behind IST, so an IST evening post reaches them in their afternoon.
- **Telugu version**: AP and Telangana plus a large, young, tech-heavy US diaspora (Texas, California, NJ, the Northeast). That audience sits 10:30–13:30 hours behind IST.
- **English version**: urban pan-India viewers plus second-generation diaspora (US, UK, Canada, Australia) who may not read Indic scripts.

### Gaps
- I could not verify ACS 2023 B16001 weighted counts for Hindi, Gujarati and Telugu (census.gov tables were not fetched). Use "Telugu fastest-growing" only with the CIS 2018 citation.
- No MEA diaspora totals by language. MEA publishes NRI/PIO totals by country, not by language.
- No figures for the Gujarati diaspora in East Africa or for Indian-language speakers in the Gulf.

## Q3. Evidence on how regional-language and devotional/mythology content performs

### Takeaway
There is qualitative and industry evidence that devotional short video is growing, and that young Indians take in bhakti content as short clips. I found no published metric for Ramayana Reels or for Instagram engagement by language. Instagram says it does not boost any content category.

### Cited Findings
- Tips Industries MD Kumar Taurani (The Week, Mar 2025): people in their **20s and 30s take in devotional content through short-form clips**, while the 40+ audience listens to full-length devotional content. He also says the devotional music market is "growing exponentially". — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/cover/2025/03/15/tips-industries-managing-director-kumar-taurani-interview.html)
- The Week's cover story (Mar 2025) is titled "Young India is turning spiritual, and it is good business". — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/cover/2025/03/15/young-indians-embracing-spirituality.amp.html)
- Open Magazine describes "faith influencers" who turn religious teaching into short Instagram Reels, tied to growth in religious travel and faith apps. The piece is qualitative. — [Open Magazine, "Faith Accompli"](https://openthemagazine.com/cover-stories/faith-accompli)
- BITKRAFT–Redseer (Oct 2025) projects India's interactive media market, including astro/devotional tech, at **$3.2B by FY30**, with devotional tech named as a growth driver. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/astro-tech-and-micro-dramas-to-lead-2-7-billion-interactive-media-surge-by-fy30-83379.htm)
- BOOM Live documents a Ram-themed Instagram account that grew from hundreds to over 1 lakh followers. It frames part of this ecosystem as politically cultivated. This is a single case, not a benchmark. — [BOOM Live Decode](https://www.boomlive.in/decode/ram-mandir-ayodhya-viral-content-creators-bjp-cultivated-sanatan-influencers-24238)
- Adam Mosseri: "We don't try to boost one type of content or suppress another." — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/instagrams-adam-mosseri-on-india-setting-global-benchmarks-in-social-media-habits-64230.htm)
- Instagram's 2025 India Year-in-Review coverage emphasised cricket, cultural crossovers and nostalgia. Mythology did not appear among the headline trends. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/social-media/what-kept-india-scrolling-in-2025-instagrams-year-in-review-reveals-the-trends-that-ruled-the-feed-85719.htm)

### Inferences
- The series' format (short, narrated, mythology) matches how the 20s–30s audience reportedly takes in devotional material. The evidence for this is interview-based, not measured.
- The niche sits close to politically charged "Sanatan influencer" content. A scholarly, Valmiki-text framing in captions can set the series apart and avoid that association. This is my inference, not a finding.

### Gaps
- No published engagement or watch-time data for mythology or devotional Reels on Instagram India, and none split by language.
- No source on Gujarati- or Telugu-specific devotional Reel performance.

## Q4. Best posting times for Indian audiences (IST), Instagram's own guidance, and diaspora overlap

### Takeaway
The published India studies are vendor blogs that disagree with each other. The most common pattern is weekday evenings, **about 6–9 PM IST**. Instagram's head says timing matters little: post a bit before followers are active, then use Insights once you have followers. An IST evening post reaches the UK and Gulf in their afternoon or evening, but reaches the US at dawn or early morning.

### Cited Findings
- SocialPilot's study of about 250,000 Reels from 30,000+ connected accounts (study dates not stated) gives these India slots in IST:
  - Monday: **7:30 am, 3 pm, 7:30 pm**
  - Thursday: **10:30 am, 11:30 am**
  - Friday: **6:30 am, 2 pm, 10:30 pm**
  - Saturday: **6 am, 7 am, 8:30 pm**
  - Tuesday, Wednesday and Sunday: no slots listed
  The page has no publication date. — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/insights/best-time-to-post-reels-on-instagram)
- Several Indian blogs (WsCube Tech, BossWallah, timetopost.co) cluster around **6–9 PM IST weekdays**. Some give Sunday 7–9 PM, and a minority give 9–10:30 AM or 11 AM–1 PM. None publishes a methodology. — [WsCube Tech](https://www.wscubetech.com/blog/instagram-post-time/); [BossWallah](https://bosswallah.com/blog/creator-hub/best-instagram-reels-posting-time-in-india-boost-views-engagement/); [timetopost.co](https://timetopost.co/best-time-to-post/instagram/india/)
- Adam Mosseri reportedly said post timing "doesn't matter much", and that posting just before followers are most active may give a small boost. He also said "prioritize quality over quantity, but in general, posting more will help." Both quotes come from secondary write-ups; I found no primary Mosseri post. — [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/ca/blog/time-post-instagram); [ManyChat](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-optimal-posting-time/)
- Later disagrees with "doesn't matter": its view is that timing matters, among other factors. — [Later](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)
- **Diaspora overlap, Nov–Dec 2026** (arithmetic from the offsets in Q2):

  | IST | UK (GMT) | US Eastern | US Pacific | UAE | Nairobi | Sydney |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 07:30 | 02:00 | 21:00 (previous day) | 18:00 (previous day) | 06:00 | 05:00 | 13:00 |
  | 12:00 | 06:30 | 01:30 | 22:30 (previous day) | 10:30 | 09:30 | 17:30 |
  | 18:30 | 13:00 | 08:00 | 05:00 | 17:00 | 16:00 | 00:00 |
  | 20:00 | 14:30 | 09:30 | 06:30 | 18:30 | 17:30 | 01:30 |
  | 21:30 | 16:00 | 11:00 | 08:00 | 20:00 | 19:00 | 03:00 |

### Inferences
- **hi**: about 7–8 PM IST, a domestic and Gulf prime-time evening.
- **gu**: about 7–8:30 PM IST. That lands in the UK and East African afternoon, and Reels keep being recommended for days.
- **te**: two options. IST evening suits AP and Telangana. About 7–8:30 AM IST lands in the US Eastern evening (8:30–10 PM, previous day) and Pacific evening (5:30–7 PM), which may suit the US diaspora. No study tests this split. It is a hypothesis to A/B test once Insights exist.
- **en**: IST evening for urban India, or about 9:30 PM IST, which is UK late afternoon and US Eastern morning.
- Reels are mostly served to non-followers. For a brand-new account with no followers, exact timing matters less than retention. Once there are 100+ followers, the "Most active times" panel in Insights should replace these defaults.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or Instagram-official India posting-time data. The Hootsuite, Sprout Social and Buffer India-specific figures were not retrieved; their studies are mostly global or US-centric.
- No data on whether devotional audiences have different active hours, such as early-morning puja time. That is plausible but unsourced.

### Takeaway
**Vivah Panchami (Rama–Sita wedding) falls on Monday 14 Dec 2026**, and sources agree. **Gita Jayanti / Mokshada Ekadashi is around 19–20 Dec 2026**, with a one-day disagreement between sources. Valmiki Jayanti (Sharad Purnima, October) has already passed.

### Cited Findings
- Vivah Panchami 2026 is **Monday 14 December**, Margashirsha Shukla Panchami (New Delhi panchang). — [HinduTone](https://hindutone.com/festivals/vivah-panchami-2026-date-ram-sita-vivah/); [Haindavam](https://haindavam.org/festivals/vivaha_panchami/2026-12-14); [Bhakti Bharat](https://www.bhaktibharat.com/festival/vivah-panchami)

The Hindu American Foundation gives **19–20 Dec** ([HAF](https://www.hinduamerican.org/hindu-holidays-guide)). [Hindu Blog](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2025/10/hindu-festivals-2026-list-of-important-festivals-calendar.html) and the [SAHMS calendar](https://sahms.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Hindu-Calender-2026.pdf) give **20 Dec**. ## Q6. Does posting several language versions of the same Reel close together help or hurt?

### Takeaway
Instagram has no official guidance on posting multiple language versions from the same account. Its published originality rules target *reposting other people's content*: duplicates get swapped for the original, and aggregator accounts can lose recommendations. Meta's own answer for multilingual reach is a single Reel with AI translation. Four near-identical Reels from one account (same visuals, different audio) are untested against Instagram's duplicate detection.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram's ranking update gives more distribution to original creators. When it detects identical content, Instagram **replaces the reposted Reel with the original** in recommendations. Accounts that repeatedly post unoriginal content can become **ineligible for recommendations**. Mosseri admitted originality is hard to know "for sure". — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html); [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/instagrams-new-ranking-system-prioritizes-original-content)
- PetaPixel (30 Apr 2026) says the reposted-content policies extend beyond Reels to photos and carousels. — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- Meta's route to a multilingual audience is **one Reel with Meta AI voice translation and lip sync**, which viewers see in their own language. It is available for en, hi and te but not gu. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/11/instagram-empowers-creators-to-go-global-with-local-voice-translations-and-fonts/)
- Mosseri: posting more generally helps, but quality comes before quantity (secondary source). — [ManyChat](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-optimal-posting-time/)

### Inferences
- The rules are aimed at reposting other creators' work. Self-made language versions with distinct audio, on-screen text and captions are probably not "unoriginal". Still, the visuals are identical, so a classifier could treat them as duplicates and show only one in recommendations. Low risk, but unproven.
- Lower-risk options:
  - Space the versions out (for example, one language per day, or each in that audience's best slot).
  - Make sure on-screen text and the first frame differ by language. The chalk cards already use each language's own script.
  - Write each caption originally in its language rather than translating.
  - Consider separate language accounts later if Insights show cannibalisation, such as one version getting recommended while the others stall.
- Publishing four Reels within minutes also splits the first-hour engagement of a zero-follower account four ways, and floods the profile grid with one topic.

### Gaps
- No official Instagram statement on same-creator multilingual duplicates, and no published test of it. This can only be settled by watching the account's own per-Reel non-follower reach after posting.
