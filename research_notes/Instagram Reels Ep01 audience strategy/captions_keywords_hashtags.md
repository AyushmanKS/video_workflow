# Instagram Reel captions, keywords and hashtags for "RAMAYANA — The Epic in Chalk" Ep01 (en / hi / gu / te), 2025–2026

Scope note: research run 2026-11-16. Instagram's own pages (help center, hashtag pages, creator posts) mostly could not be fetched directly. Where a claim rests on secondary coverage, these notes say so. Counts from third-party hashtag tools are tool estimates, not Instagram data.

## 1. Official guidance: is caption text indexed for search? Keywords vs hashtags, the hashtag cap, and whether hashtags still help reach

### Takeaway
Instagram's official position is that Search matches the text people type against usernames, display names, bios, captions, hashtags and places. Keywords and hashtags belong in the caption, not in comments. Since 18–19 December 2025 the limit is 5 hashtags per post or Reel, and Instagram recommends a few targeted tags over generic ones. Mosseri has said repeatedly that hashtags do not meaningfully increase reach; they help with categorisation and search.

### Cited Findings
- Official (Aug 2021, older but still Instagram's main published explainer): "The text you enter in the search bar is by far the most important signal for Search." Instagram matches it against usernames, bios, captions, hashtags and places. Popularity signals (clicks, likes, shares, follows) are used when many results match. — [Instagram: Breaking Down How Instagram Search Works](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works/)
- Official creator tip from the same post: "For a post to be found in Search, put keywords and hashtags in the caption, not the comments." Also: "Search results are matched by text", so pick a handle and profile name that relate to your content. — [Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works/)
- Hashtag cap: Instagram said it "will gradually update the number of hashtags that you can include in a caption for a reel or post to five". It added: "We find that using fewer (up to 5) more targeted hashtags, rather than many generic ones" improves performance. The cap is meant to curb spam with generic tags such as #reels and #explore. Reported 19 Dec 2025. — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-hashtags-five-per-post-10923075); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)
- Business Today attributes the announcement to Instagram's @creators account (dated 19 Dec 2025, saying "today"). The limit fell from 30 to 5, and some users saw a 3-tag cap during testing. It reports Mosseri "recently reiterated" that hashtags are not the main driver of reach, and that recommendations rely on "caption keywords, on-screen text, and visual content". It gives no direct Mosseri quote for the last point. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/no-more-hashtag-stuffing-instagram-limits-hashtag-usage-to-just-5-per-reel-and-post-507444-2025-12-19)
- Mosseri quote, as relayed by secondary sources citing Social Media Today: "Hashtags are no longer a primary way to increase your reach on Instagram. They don't significantly increase your reach." I did not see the primary video. — [Phable summary](https://www.phable.io/phable-labs/instagram-hashtags-keywords-discovery); [Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/instagram-search-seo/)
- Sources disagree on whether the 5-tag cap counts caption and comments together. Some reports say splitting tags across caption and comments no longer adds slots. One guide quotes the help center as "You can use up to 5 tags on a post… If you include more than 5 tags… your comment won't post." This is unconfirmed against the live help center. — [Social Media Today search summary](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/); [InstantDM](https://instantdm.com/blog/instagram-hashtags)
- Several 2025–26 secondary sources say public posts from professional accounts have been indexable by Google since July 2025, so caption text can also surface in web search. This is secondary only. — [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-posts-are-now-showing-up-in-google-search); [Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/instagram-search-seo/)
- Older experiment (2022, small sample): keyword-led captions reached more people than hashtag-led ones. — [Hootsuite experiment](https://blog.hootsuite.com/experiment-instagram-seo-vs-hashtags/)
- Official ranking explainer (31 May 2023, Mosseri): Reels ranking uses viewer activity (liked, saved, reshared, commented), history with the poster, "information about the reel… such as the audio track or visuals", and information about the poster. It predicts reshare, watch-to-end, like and audio-page visit. Watermarks, majority-text content, low resolution and reposted content get lower visibility. It says nothing specific about captions. — [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)

### Inferences
- Treat the caption as the search document. Put the exact phrases a person would type ("Valmiki", "Ramayana", "first shloka", "Ma Nishada", "krauncha bird") in the first lines as plain words, not only as hashtags.
- Use at most 5 hashtags, all in the caption. Pick specific tags (#ramayana #valmiki), not generic ones (#reels #explore #viral), which Instagram itself names as spam signals.
- Hashtags are a categorisation and search aid, not a reach lever. Reach comes from watch time, sends and likes (see Q2).
- The pipeline's on-screen chalk text and narration probably feed content understanding ("visuals"), but no official source confirms that on-screen text is OCR-indexed for Search. That claim is secondary only.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the live help center text on hashtag limits or comments, or the original @creators/Threads post. The exact wording and the caption-plus-comment scope are unverified.
- I found no official 2024–2026 update to the 2021 search explainer, so whether audio transcripts or on-screen text are searchable is unconfirmed.

## 2. Caption length, first-line hook, line breaks, emojis and CTAs for sends and saves

### Takeaway
Mosseri named "sends per reach" (DM shares divided by reach) as one of the most important ranking signals. Secondary reporting lists watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the top three, with sends weighted more for non-followers. The caption's job is therefore (a) a search-matching, curiosity-driven first line that survives truncation and (b) a natural reason to send the Reel to someone. Caption length itself matters little for Reels engagement, according to the one recent dataset found.

### Cited Findings
- Mosseri (Reel quoted July 2024): "One of the most important signals we use in ranking is sends per reach… out of all the people who saw your video or photo, how many of them sent it to a friend in a DM?" — [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389)
- Secondary sources attribute to Mosseri (Jan 2025) the top three signals: watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Likes are "slightly more important for connected content" and sends "slightly more important for unconnected content". The "3–5× weight for sends" figure appears only in marketing blogs and is unverified. — [SocialPilot Reels algorithm](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm); [Hanami summary of public Meta statements](https://hanami-social-website.pages.dev/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/)
- Truncation: the feed cuts captions at roughly 125 characters before "more". Instagram does not publish this threshold; it comes from guides. Guides say the Reels tab overlay shows only about 55–60 characters, which is unverified. — [Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/insights/instagram-character-limit?hsLang=en); [ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/instagram-reels-captions.html)
- The 2,200-character caption maximum is widely reported by tool vendors. — [Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/insights/instagram-character-limit?hsLang=en)
- PostPlanify's study of 4,408 posts from 152 accounts (Apr–Jul 2026) found caption length "barely matters" for Reels engagement but matters for feed posts. This is a vendor study, not peer-reviewed. — [PostPlanify](https://postplanify.com/blog/instagram-caption-length)
- Older (2022): Hootsuite's Reels test compared long (125+ word) and one-line captions on 5 videos each, a tiny sample. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/experiment-what-reels-caption-length-gets-the-best-engagement)

### Inferences
- Line 1, under about 55 characters so it survives the Reels overlay, should hold the hook plus the primary keyword, e.g. "The Ramayana began with a bird's death" or "Valmiki's first shloka: Ma Nishada". The existing gu/te post.txt titles ("રામાયણની શરૂઆત એક ઋષિનાં આંસુથી થઈ | રામાયણ ભાગ 1") already follow this pattern. Keep the series marker short.
- Lines 2–4 (about 125–300 characters) should cover the factual context with the secondary keywords (Valmiki, krauncha, Tamasa, Brahma, Adi Kavi, first verse of Sanskrit poetry), in short lines with line breaks.
- The send-oriented CTA should be specific and respectful, e.g. "Send this to someone who loves the Ramayana" or "Share with the person who told you Ramayana stories as a child". Add a save CTA ("Save for Ep02") to signal the series. Use one CTA, possibly two; avoid engagement bait like "comment 'Jai Shri Ram' or…", which Instagram's recommendation guidelines treat as low quality. That last point is inferred and was not verified in this run.
- Use one or two emojis at most (e.g. 🪔 🙏). No evidence found that emojis help reach. With devotional content, a sparing, respectful tone is safer.

### Gaps
- No primary Meta source on the Reels caption preview length.
- No 2025–26 data specific to Indian-language or devotional Reels on caption length, CTAs or emojis.

## 3. Script choice: native script vs romanized, and bilingual captions

### Takeaway
Instagram supports hashtags and captions in Devanagari and other Indic scripts. In 2026 Instagram/Edits added Devanagari fonts and Hindi bilingual auto-captions, which signals investment in native-script content. However, there is no official documentation on whether Search cross-matches native script with romanized queries ("రామాయణం" vs "ramayanam"). Search is text-matched, so the safest pattern is native-script caption body plus a romanized/English keyword line.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram Search is text-matched against captions and hashtags. The 2021 explainer says nothing about transliteration. — [Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works/)
- Edits app (2026): Devanagari and Bengali–Assamese fonts for Reels text and captions, filtered by device language, plus auto bilingual captions with Hindi as a second-language option. — [PRmoment India](https://www.prmoment.in/pr-news/instagrams-multilingual-push-is-a-quiet-but-significant-shift-for-indias-communicators); [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/?p=2482590)
- Instagram previously introduced local voice translations and Indian fonts in India. — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/instagram-introduces-local-voice-translations-and-fonts-in-india-149757.html)
- How-to guides report that Hindi-script hashtags work and are widely used (e.g. #शायरी, #हिंदी), and that Explore suggests related native-script tags. Usernames must be Latin, but display names can be Devanagari. These are guides, not official sources. — [Transliterate.Blog guide](https://all-blogs-8rl.pages.dev/Transliterate.Blog/guides--type-hindi-in-instagram)
- Blogs claim Hinglish "performs best" in India and that 65% of creators prefer it, with 20–30% higher engagement. These figures have no named study and are unverified, so do not rely on them. — [deepaksinghh India report](https://deepaksinghh.github.io/instagram-india-mega-report)
- Spelling variance: both #ramayan and #ramayana are used as separate tags, so people search both spellings. — [displaypurposes #ramayan](https://displaypurposes.com/hashtags/hashtag/ramayan); [displaypurposes #ramayana](https://displaypurposes.com/hashtags/hashtag/ramayana)

### Inferences
- Hindi, Gujarati and Telugu captions: write the hook and body in native script, which matches native-script queries and reads as authentic to the target audience. Add one romanized keyword line (e.g. "Valmiki Ramayana | Ma Nishada | Krauncha") so romanized and English queries also match. Romanized searching is very common on Indian keyboards. This is an inference; no data was found.
- Hashtags per regional version: mix one or two native-script tags (#रामायण / #રામાયણ / #రామాయణం) with two or three Latin tags (#ramayana #valmiki, plus #ramayanam for Telugu). This covers both query scripts within the 5-tag cap.
- Keep English lines minimal (one line) so the caption does not look machine-translated and the hook stays native. A full parallel English translation pushes the native text below the fold.

### Gaps
- No official or measured evidence on Instagram cross-script or transliteration matching, or on relative search volume for native versus romanized terms in any of the three languages.

## 5. On-screen text, Reel title and alt text

### Takeaway
Instagram's official Reels ranking uses "visuals" and audio. Secondary reporting says on-screen text feeds understanding, but no official confirmation was found that on-screen text is search-indexed. Custom alt text exists in the app, and through the API it is supported only for image posts (since 24 Mar 2025). Reels and stories are not supported, so the pipeline cannot set alt text on Reels via the API.

### Cited Findings
- Reels ranking uses "information about the reel… such as the audio track or visuals". Majority-text content and watermarks reduce visibility. — [Instagram Ranking Explained (2023)](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)
- Instagram introduced custom alternative text so creators can write their own image descriptions. — [Instagram: Advancing Accessibility](https://about.instagram.com/blog/tips-and-tricks/advancing-accessibility-on-instagram)
- Meta developer docs: the `alt_text` field was added for image posts on `/<IG_ID>/media` on 24 Mar 2025, and Reels and stories are not supported. Reels are published with `media_type=REELS` and `video_url`. — [Meta Content Publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/); [IG User Media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media)
- In-app alt text paths described by universities ("Advanced settings" / "Write alt text", or "..." then "Edit" then "Add alt text" on existing posts) are documented for photos. A Reels-specific path is unverified. — [Utah State](https://www.usu.edu/accessibility/social-media/instagram.php); [Bowdoin](https://bowdoin.teamdynamix.com/TDClient/1814/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=99761)
- Business Today reports, without a direct quote, that recommendations prioritise "caption keywords, on-screen text, and visual content". — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/no-more-hashtag-stuffing-instagram-limits-hashtag-usage-to-just-5-per-reel-and-post-507444-2025-12-19)

### Inferences
- Put the episode keyword in the chalk title card in each language's script (e.g. "रामायण — भाग 1"). This helps content classification at no cost. Avoid text-heavy frames, which Instagram demotes.
- The cover/thumbnail text should match line 1 of the caption for consistency.
- Do not rely on alt text for Reels. If it is wanted, add it manually in the app after publishing, if the option appears.

### Gaps
- No official statement on OCR or speech-transcript indexing for Search. No confirmed in-app alt-text option for Reels found.

## 6. Comment pinning and first-comment hashtags

### Takeaway
First-comment hashtags are counter to official guidance. Instagram says to put keywords and hashtags in the caption, not comments, for Search, and the 2025 cap may count comment tags too. A pinned comment is useful for engagement (series navigation, a question), not for discovery.

### Cited Findings
- "For a post to be found in Search, put keywords and hashtags in the caption, not the comments." — [Instagram (2021)](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works/)
- Hootsuite: only captions are searchable, so keywords belong in captions. — [Hootsuite Instagram SEO](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-seo)
- Reports conflict on whether the 5-tag cap spans caption and comments. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/); [InstantDM](https://instantdm.com/blog/instagram-hashtags)
- Only one source (Kontentino) claims comments are read as a search signal, which conflicts with official guidance. — [Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/instagram-search-seo/)

### Inferences
- Drop first-comment hashtags. Pin an owner comment such as a short "Next: Ep02 …" line, or a question about the shloka's meaning, to seed comments. Respond to early comments, since comments are an engagement signal in the 2023 ranking explainer ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)).

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 primary source on whether pinned comments affect distribution.

## 7. Sensitivities for devotional content

### Takeaway
Avoid clickbait that misrepresents scripture or mocks deities and sages. Attribute the episode to the Valmiki Ramayana (Bala Kanda), and present interpretive claims as "according to Valmiki's Ramayana". Indian law (BNS s.299, formerly IPC 295A) penalises deliberate, malicious acts meant to outrage religious feelings. Courts require malicious intent, but a single FIR can still be costly, so a respectful, source-attributed tone is a practical risk control.

### Cited Findings
- BNS s.299 replaces IPC s.295A and covers deliberate, malicious acts intended to outrage religious feelings. Commentary says it reaches social-media posts and videos. — [Drishti IAS Law](https://law.drishtiias.com/current-affairs/social-media-posts-and-communal-harmony); [ApniLaw bare act](https://www.apnilaw.com/bare-act/bns/section-299-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-bns-deliberate-and-malicious-acts-intended-to-outrage-religious-feelings-of-any-class-by-insulting-its-religion-or-religious-beliefs)
- Courts have held that careless or unwitting acts do not meet the bar, but critics note proceedings can be triggered by a single FIR. — [SuperKalam (Sept 2026 editorial summary)](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/17-09-2026/let-s-curb-misuse-of-a-legal-relic-that-elevates-the-mob-over-the-individual-pg14-d9315685-6d35-42a8-894b-d1b53b4f3292); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/judiciary/iftar-on-ganga-to-mf-husain-paintings-the-elasticity-of-hurting-religious-sentiments-in-indian-courts/2935081/)
- Story facts for accuracy: Valmiki at the Tamasa saw a hunter kill a male krauncha during mating; the female's grief moved him to the first verse ("Ma nishada…"); Brahma then instructed him to compose the Ramayana. — [ThePrint excerpt](https://theprint.in/pageturner/excerpt/narada-used-sanskrit-grammar-turn-valmikis-curse-into-blessing/2282184); [Hindutone](https://hindutone.com/spirituality/valmiki-ramayana-complete-story-lord-rama-chapter-chapter/)

### Inferences
- Do: honorifics (Maharishi Valmiki / महर्षि वाल्मीकि / వాల్మీకి మహర్షి), "according to Valmiki's Ramayana", the accurate shloka text in the caption (a natural save trigger), and a calm tone.
- Avoid: "the Ramayana is fake/a myth" framing, "shocking secret Valmiki hid", "you won't believe" hype, and politicised tags (e.g. #hindutva, which shows up as a related tag) on a storytelling series. Also avoid casual "dacoit" framing of Valmiki. The "Ratnakar the robber" origin is a later tradition, not in the Valmiki text itself (common scholarly view, not verified in this run). The project's hook "hunter-sage" should be checked against the script's research notes.
- Use "mythology" carefully. Some devout viewers object to calling the epic "mythology". English tags like #hindumythology are widely used, but the caption body can say "the epic" / "Itihasa" instead. This is an inference; no survey data found.

### Gaps
- No platform-specific (Meta) policy found on devotional content beyond general Community Guidelines. No data on audience reaction to "mythology" wording.
