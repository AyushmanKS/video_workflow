# Test Indic-Speak first, then fix QA alignment

The single highest-value change for shorts-studio is to A/B test **Indic-Speak** (Bodhan AI / AI4Bharat, Sept 2026) against Veena kavya. Indic-Speak is the only freely self-hostable model found that covers **all ten target languages plus Sanskrit**, ships a working **4-bit MLX port of about 1.8 GB**, and uses the same SNAC codec family as Veena, so most of the existing integration code should carry over. Its license (Indic Open Model License plus the Llama 3.2 Community License) allows self-hosted commercial output, which covers monetized YouTube. The best-measured Indian TTS, **Maya 2 Native** from Veena's own makers (Apache-2.0, hi CER 1.52%), officially runs only on Linux with NVIDIA CUDA, so it stays on a watch list. For English, Kokoro af_heart is still competitive; **Chatterbox Multilingual V3** (MIT) and **Magpie-TTS Multilingual 357M** are the only commercially usable challengers worth testing. On the QA side, Whisper is a speech *recognizer*, not a voice. It should be replaced for Indic languages by **AI4Bharat IndicConformer-600M** (MIT, 22 languages), whose CTC head also does **forced alignment** of the known script for subtitles. The usual default aligner (MMS-FA) is non-commercial and must be avoided. Across the rest of the pipeline the biggest wins are **FLUX.2 [klein] 4B** (Apache-2.0) for reference-locked characters, **depth parallax plus vector stroke order** for animating stills, and **ACE-Step 1.5** (MIT, MLX) for music beds. Most "awesome" Claude Code subagent collections add little to the studio's twelve purpose-built agents. One caveat runs through all of it: none of the quality figures below comes from an independent listening test or from a measurement on an M5 Air, so every recommendation ends in a local test, not a switch.

## Indic-Speak covers every target language; Maya 2 Native has the best numbers but needs CUDA

The Indic open-weight TTS field has moved a long way since Veena shipped in mid-2025. Veena itself has not: it is still a **3B Llama-architecture model with a SNAC 24 kHz codec, English and Hindi only**, last modified October 2025, with no v2 ([Veena card](https://huggingface.co/maya-research/Veena); [HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/maya-research/Veena)). Its makers have since released **Maya 2 Native**: 2.5B parameters, Apache-2.0, 11 Indic languages plus English, 20 voices and zero-shot cloning. It reports the strongest evidence of any open model, with ASR CER of **hi 1.52%, mr 1.66%, te 2.29%, kn 2.28%, pa 2.51%, bn 2.60%, ml 3.75%, ta 4.42%**, and it ranks #2 among real-time Hindi models on VoiceArena, ahead of Sarvam's commercial Bulbul-V3. Two facts disqualify it for now. The card states it needs **Linux plus CUDA 12.8 and "does not establish CPU, Apple MPS or browser inference support,"** and it has **no Sanskrit** ([Maya 2 Native card](https://huggingface.co/maya-research/maya2-native)). These are self-reported numbers with no human MOS study.

**Indic-Speak** is the practical successor. It is Llama-3.2-3B-based, covers **22 Indic languages plus English including Sanskrit**, and has 44 Rasa-studio voices (one female and one male per language), every one of which can speak every language ([HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/bodhan-ai/indic-speak); [voices.md](https://huggingface.co/iniquitous/indic-speak-mlx-4bit/raw/main/voices.md)). AI4Bharat's own IndicF5 and Indic Parler-TTS cards now recommend it over those models ([IndicF5 card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/IndicF5)). The community MLX 4-bit port runs at **RTF about 1.0–1.2 on an M3 Max** and takes uppercase emotion tags (ANGER, HAPPY, SAD, FEAR) plus a seed. The port also carries a warning that matters for this pipeline: quantized variants **"tend to stop early or drift on multi-sentence prompts,"** so narration must be chunked by sentence, and quality was checked by ear on Hindi only ([MLX card](https://huggingface.co/iniquitous/indic-speak-mlx-4bit)). Expect a fanless Air to run slower than the M3 Max, roughly 1.5–2.5× real time. That figure is an estimate.

The license needs one careful reading. The Indic Open Model License grants royalty-free rights to "use, reproduce, … sell, deploy," treats synthesized audio as Output, and restricts mainly **hosting the model for third parties** and products above 500M MAU or US$250M revenue. It forbids impersonating a real voice without consent, and it requires you to indemnify the licensor for your Output ([license text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Bodhan-AI/bodhan-model-info/main/licenses/indic-open-model-license/v1/Indic_Open_Model_License.md)). Publishing narration is not distributing the model, so self-hosted use for a monetized channel appears allowed. Adding "Built with Indic-Speak from Bodhan AI / AI4Bharat" to descriptions costs nothing and removes the ambiguity.

The fallbacks fill specific gaps. **IndicF5** (MIT, 0.4B, 11 languages, no Sanskrit) needs a reference clip plus its transcript on every call. That is a weakness for convenience but a strength for speaker consistency, because the narrator identity is pinned to one recording ([IndicF5 card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/IndicF5)). **Indic Parler-TTS** (Apache-2.0, 0.9B) is voiced by text description, has 69 named speakers, and publishes the only per-language naturalness figures: Native Speaker Score **te 88.5, kn 88.2, ml 86.5, bn 86.2, hi 84.8, mr 77.0, ta 75.5, gu 75.4, sa 99.8** ([Indic Parler card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts)). **Svara** (Apache-2.0, Orpheus-3B, 19 languages including Sanskrit) has a 1.9 GB MLX 4-bit port and end-of-sentence emotion tags, but offers only one voice per language and gender and publishes no benchmarks ([Svara card](https://huggingface.co/kenpath/svara-tts-v1); [MLX port](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/svara-tts-v1-4bit)). **SPRING_F5** from IIT Madras (Apache-2.0, 23 Indic languages including Sanskrit, updated Nov 2026) is a fresh F5 fine-tune worth a later look ([SPRING_F5 card](https://huggingface.co/SPRINGLab/SPRING_F5)).

Three license traps narrow the field. **Indic-Mio** is labelled Apache-2.0 but trained its English side on **Expresso, which is CC-BY-NC** ([Indic-Mio card](https://huggingface.co/SPRINGLab/Indic-Mio); [Expresso](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/ylacombe/expresso)). **Meta MMS-TTS** and the Sanskrit-branded **Samskriti-Svara** are CC-BY-NC outright ([MMS](https://huggingface.co/api/models/facebook/mms-tts-hin); [Samskriti-Svara](https://huggingface.co/Shivam6566/Samskriti-Svara)). Sarvam has released **no open TTS weights**; Bulbul is API-only ([Sarvam HF](https://huggingface.co/sarvamai)).

## Sanskrit shlokas need their own voice clip, not the Hindi narrator

Only Indic Parler publishes a Sanskrit quality number (**NSS 99.79**, speaker Aryan, with Sanskrit emotion support). Indic-Speak has dedicated Sanskrit voices, Bharati and Aryaman, while Maya 2 Native and IndicF5 do not support Sanskrit at all ([Indic Parler card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts); [voices.md](https://huggingface.co/iniquitous/indic-speak-mlx-4bit/raw/main/voices.md)). The Parler score almost certainly reflects read speech, not metrical recitation with correct svara and caesura pauses, and **no model has a published test of anusvara, visarga, sandhi compounds or chandas**. The workable design keeps the Hindi narrator (Veena or Indic-Speak Kavya) for narration and renders each verse as a separate clip in a Sanskrit voice. The verse clip is then exempt from the whole-narration `speaker_consistency` check, or that check gets a declared second speaker. The alternative is to let Indic-Speak's cross-lingual voices keep one timbre across both. Which choice wins is an open question for the owner's ear.

## General-purpose TTS stops at Hindi; Kokoro stays unless Chatterbox or Magpie beat it

Among general multilingual models, **none lists Marathi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam or Punjabi** except OmniVoice, whose weights are CC-BY-NC ([OmniVoice](https://huggingface.co/k2-fsa/OmniVoice)). The strongest-sounding broad models all fail the commercial test. **Fish Audio S2 Pro** (top open weights on Artificial Analysis, Elo 1121) is research/non-commercial. **Higgs Audio v3** is non-commercial. **Voxtral TTS** is CC BY-NC because of its reference voices. **IndexTTS-2** requires a bilibili agreement ([Fish blog](https://fish.audio/blog/what-we-mean-by-open-source-for-s2/); [Higgs v3](https://huggingface.co/bosonai/higgs-audio-v3-tts-4b); [Voxtral](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-4B-TTS-2603); [IndexTTS](https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts)). **Qwen3-TTS** and **CosyVoice 3** are Apache but have no Hindi ([arXiv 2601.15621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15621)).

That leaves three commercially usable English challengers to Kokoro, whose Elo of about **1056** sits just below Magpie's **1071** on the open-weight board ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard)). **Chatterbox Multilingual V3** (MIT, 0.5B) adds expressive "exaggeration" control, zero-shot cloning and a dedicated Hindi finetune. Its output carries an inaudible PerTh watermark, and a reference clip in the wrong language leaks its accent into the output ([Chatterbox card](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox)). **Magpie-TTS Multilingual 357M** (NVIDIA Open Model License, which permits commercial use; read the full text) has MLX 4-bit, CoreML and GGUF ports ([Magpie](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/magpie_tts_multilingual_357m); [MLX 4-bit](https://huggingface.co/aufklarer/Magpie-TTS-Multilingual-357M-MLX-4bit)). **VoxCPM2** (Apache-2.0, 2B, 48 kHz, voice design from text) lists CUDA but is supported in mlx-audio ([VoxCPM2](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2)). All three run through **mlx-audio** on Apple Silicon ([mlx-audio](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio)). For Hindi, Kokoro's four voices are all **grade C** and rely on espeak-ng G2P ([VOICES.md](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md)), so Veena, not Kokoro, is the Hindi bar. The goal of "one cloned narrator across all languages" has no general-purpose answer in November 2026. The closest route pairs an Indic model with Chatterbox's bundled voice-conversion script to map output onto one timbre, and that route is untested.

## Whisper is the ear, not the voice: IndicConformer plus CTC alignment fixes QA and subtitles

Whisper (faster-whisper large-v3-turbo in the current QA) transcribes audio. It cannot speak. Its weakness for this pipeline shows up in two places. Whisper's default normalizer strips matras and virama, which inflates Indic WER ([arXiv 2409.02449](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02449v4.pdf)). And Indic-trained models now beat it by wide margins. On the independent **Voice of India** benchmark (15 languages, 536 h of unscripted speech), **IndicConformer scored OI-WER hi 8.2, bn 10.7**, while Meta's Omnilingual LLM-7B managed hi 13.7 and Telugu 50.7 ([Voice of India](https://voiceofindiablogs.ai.joshtalks.com/)).

The recommended QA ASR is **IndicConformer-600M-multilingual**: **MIT**, 22 languages, hybrid CTC + RNNT, loads through `transformers` with `trust_remote_code`, and is gated behind a terms click ([model card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual)). A CoreML RNNT conversion exists for seven languages, but it has no timestamps ([CoreML port](https://huggingface.co/phequals/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual-coreml-rnnt)). The likely more accurate **Indic-Transcribe-core** (Canary-1B-v2 base, 25 languages **including Sanskrit**) reports an average OI-WER of **8.7, with hi 3.5 and mr 5.7**. These are vendor numbers, and its license adds share-alike for derivatives and conditions inherited from Canary ([Indic-Transcribe-core](https://huggingface.co/bodhan-ai/indic-transcribe-core)). Internal QA use appears fine, but read the terms first. **SraVaani** (IISc, MIT, 65 languages; the 0.5-live CTC variant includes Sanskrit) is the third MIT option ([SraVaani-0.5-live](https://huggingface.co/ARTPARK-IISc/SraVaani-0.5-live); [LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/iisc-team-releases-sravaani-asr-for-65-indian-languages-a6aa9c5b)). Keep faster-whisper for English.

Subtitles should come from **forced alignment**, which runs Viterbi decoding over CTC log-probs against the script the pipeline already has, not from ASR word timestamps. The trap is that the popular aligner checkpoints, **MMS-300M / torchaudio MMS_FA, are CC-BY-NC**, and the ctc-forced-aligner package defaults to one even though its code is BSD ([HF checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/MahmoudAshraf/mms-300m-1130-forced-aligner); [ctc-forced-aligner](https://www.github.com/MahmoudAshraf97/ctc-forced-aligner)). WhisperX ships Indic align models only for hi, te, ml and ur, and their licenses are unverified ([whisperX alignment.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/m-bain/whisperX/main/whisperx/alignment.py)). The clean stack feeds **IndicConformer's CTC head** (or SraVaani's) into ctc-forced-aligner's alignment utilities, NeMo Forced Aligner, or `torchaudio.functional.forced_align`. That last function is deprecated since 2.8, so pin torchaudio ≤ 2.9 if you use it ([torchaudio docs](https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/2.8.0/generated/torchaudio.functional.forced_align.html)). Align **one scene at a time**, because NFA with IndicConformer degrades on long sequences ([arXiv 2411.04699](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04699)).

Pronunciation QA can then go beyond CER at no extra model cost. First, normalize both script and transcript: numbers to words with `indic-numtowords`, folded nukta, or AI4Bharat's open OI-WER variant lattice. This removes false alarms such as "digits vs words" ([arXiv 2609.20504](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20504); [Voice of India](https://voiceofindiablogs.ai.joshtalks.com/)). Second, at each aspirated grapheme (ख घ छ झ ठ ढ थ ध फ भ), compare the aligned CTC posterior for the scripted character against its unaspirated twin, and flag the frames where the twin wins. The output is a **confusion-pair report (थ→त, ध→द)** aimed squarely at Veena's known aspiration and first-word-onset weakness. No off-the-shelf open Indic pronunciation-assessment model exists. The universal IPA recognizer `wav2vec2-xlsr-53-espeak-cv-ft` can serve as a second opinion, though its license is reported inconsistently as MIT or Apache ([HF](https://huggingface.co/facebook/wav2vec2-xlsr-53-espeak-cv-ft)). For Sanskrit, ASR is weak (IndicWhisper **48% WER**), so judge verses by per-syllable alignment confidence against the known shloka text, not by CER ([Vistaar paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15386)).

## Reference-locked images and cheap 2.5D motion beat video diffusion on 16 GB

**Character drift** (bearded vs clean-shaven, adult vs child) is the most visible defect, and the fix is reference conditioning. **FLUX.2 [klein] 4B** is Apache-2.0, runs in mflux, and takes **up to 4 reference images** natively. Its 9B sibling is non-commercial ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B); [mflux](https://github.com/filipstrand/mflux)). The workflow is to generate one canonical character sheet per recurring figure with Z-Image Turbo (Apache-2.0, the current model), then render every scene through klein 4B with "same sage as reference." Phase two trains a small per-character LoRA with `mflux-train` on Z-Image base or klein base 4B ([mflux v0.16.0](https://newreleases.io/project/github/filipstrand/mflux/release/v.0.16.0)). **Qwen-Image-Edit-2511** (Apache-2.0, 20B, MLX quantizations available) is a heavier editing alternative ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511)). Several 2026 models are traps or carry conditions. **Qwen-Image 2.1** is research-only. **FLUX.1 Kontext dev** is non-commercial to run. **Krea 2** and **FLUX 3 Klein** are revenue-capped at $1M and $5M ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/09/21/alibaba-qwen-releases-qwen-image-2-1/); [Kontext](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-Kontext-dev); [Krea 2](https://huggingface.co/krea/Krea-2-Turbo)). Unwanted borders and extra figures are partly a distillation side-effect: turbo models ignore CFG and negative prompts, so testing the base variants with negatives, or adding an edit-model cleanup pass, is the cheap experiment. There is still no MLX IP-Adapter or InstantID.

For **animation**, two lightweight techniques fit the chalk style better than diffusion. **Depth parallax** takes a depth map from mflux's built-in Depth Pro or **Depth Anything V2 Small** (Apache-2.0; Base, Large and Giant are CC-BY-NC), splits the still into 2–3 layers, inpaints behind the foreground, and moves a 2.5D camera, which upgrades Ken Burns to real parallax ([DA-V2 Small card](https://huggingface.co/depth-anything/Depth-Anything-V2-Small/raw/main/README.md)). **True stroke order** skeletonizes the chalk lines, vectorizes them with **VTracer (MIT)** or Potrace (GPL, called as a separate CLI), sorts the paths, and redraws them with the existing chalk texture and SFX. This replaces mask reveals with drawing that looks hand-made ([raster-to-vector comparison](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_raster_to_vector_conversion_software)). Real image-to-video is now possible through **ltx-2-mlx** (MIT code; LTX-2.5 int8 with `--low-ram` on 16 GB). A 241-frame I2V clip took **563–701 s on an M1 Max**, and LTX weights are free below **$10M revenue** ([ltx-2-mlx](https://github.com/dgrauet/ltx-2-mlx); [LTX license](https://ltx.io/model/license)). On an Air, plan on 1–3 hero shots per video at most, each run alone under the progress-reporting protocol. Test first/last-frame conditioning from the chalk still so the model does not "realize" the chalk into photoreal footage. **Remotion** is free for teams of three or fewer, but it adds a React toolchain the Python chalk engine does not need ([Remotion license](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md)).

For **music**, **ACE-Step 1.5** is the only verified clean option. Code and weights are **MIT**, the card says generated music is usable "for commercial purposes," and it has an official MLX/macOS path. On 8–16 GB it recommends the 2B turbo DiT with the 0.6B LM ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/ACE-Step/Ace-Step1.5); [repo](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)). Clone it at a pinned commit rather than piping its install script into `sh`. Skip XL, which needs at least 12 GB. Raga accuracy is undocumented, so keep the numpy tanpura as a fallback or underlay. Stable Audio Open is capped at $1M revenue, and YuE2, MusicGen and HunyuanVideo-Foley are non-commercial or territory-restricted ([Stability license](https://stability.ai/license); [YuE](https://github.com/multimodal-art-projection/YuE); [Hunyuan Foley](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-Foley/blob/main/LICENSE)). Procedural chalk SFX are already license-clean, so leave them alone at the approved `CHALK_SFX_GAIN = 0.035`. Pixabay music can still trigger Content ID claims despite its free license ([foximusic](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/need-background-music-that-wont-trigger-content-id/)).

## Research, publishing and Claude Code tooling need small fixes, not new platforms

**Trend research** has fewer free sources than it used to. **pytrends was archived on 17 April 2025**, the official Google Trends API is still invite-only, and the trendspy fork scrapes undocumented endpoints ([DEV](https://dev.to/reliableapps/google-trends-api-alternatives-in-2026-getting-trends-data-as-json-and-what-to-do-about-429s-2af0)). Reddit's free API tier is **non-commercial only and needs approval** ([data365](https://data365.co/blog/reddit-api-pricing)). YouTube `search.list` reportedly has its own roughly **100-call/day bucket** ([Blotato](https://www.blotato.com/blog/youtube-api-pricing)). The sound stack stays YouTube Data API plus Wikipedia pageviews plus the vendored `yt-viral` skill, with caching. Add trendspy only as a best-effort source wrapped so a failure cannot break the stage, and apply for the Trends alpha.

For **publishing**, keep `publish.py`. Google's live `videos.insert` reference now says unverified projects are **not restricted to private**, and uploads draw from a separate **100-call/day bucket at 1 unit each**, but third-party guides still conflict, so read `privacyStatus` back after every upload ([videos.insert](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/insert); [clipember](https://clipember.com/guides/youtube-upload-comes-out-private)). For **analytics**, use **engagedViews**, which since March 2025 keeps the old Shorts view definition ([revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/revision_history)). Thumbnail impressions and CTR live in the bulk **Reporting API**, which takes one job creation and then daily CSVs ([Reporting metrics](https://developers.google.com/youtube/reporting/v1/reports/metrics)). Postiz is AGPL and needs Node/Postgres/Redis, which is too heavy for no gain ([Postiz](https://github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app)).

For **video QA**, learned quality scorers carry legal risk. DOVER's code is under the non-commercial S-Lab License and its weight license is disputed, and Q-Align ships an S-Lab file too ([DOVER #43](https://github.com/VQAssessment/DOVER/issues/43); [Q-Align](https://github.com/q-future/q-align)). Use classic checks instead: PySceneDetect (scene cuts vs planned timings), an ffmpeg blur or `signalstats` check for chalk legibility, a subtitle-vs-alignment timing check, and CLIP/DINO similarity between each scene's figure and its character sheet before G2. The PySceneDetect and VMAF licenses were not verified in this research.

For **Claude Code**, the official sources are `anthropics/skills` and `anthropics/claude-plugins-official`. The useful parts, `skill-creator` and the document skills, are already available here ([anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills)). **VoltAgent/awesome-claude-code-subagents** (MIT, "we do not audit") and **wshobson/agents** are coding-oriented and duplicate nothing the studio lacks ([VoltAgent](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-claude-code-subagents)). Official **Remotion skills** matter only if rendering moves to Remotion ([Tella guide](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-use-remotion-agent-skills-with-claude-code)). The better investment is project-native. Build a `tts-ab` skill and harness that wraps the tests below. Add a **PostToolUse or pre-render hook** that blocks rendering until the alignment and confusion-pair QA pass. Add a **pre-commit hook** that enforces the existing secrets/media scan. Vendor any third-party skill as a single reviewed SKILL.md pinned to a SHA, as with the yt-* skills, and never install one that requests YouTube OAuth, since that could bypass G3.

Items 1 and 2 come first because they are fully MIT/BSD, need no voice change, and **create the measuring stick** that items 3, 4 and 8 depend on. Without aspiration-aware, normalized QA, a TTS A/B test reduces to impressions.

## Conclusion

The center of gravity for Indian-language speech has moved from general-purpose labs to Indian institutions. AI4Bharat, Bodhan AI, IIT Madras, IISc and Maya Research now publish models that beat the global multilingual releases on Indic languages, and those global releases mostly stop at Hindi or forbid commercial use. The binding constraint for this studio is therefore **hardware, not licensing or quality**. The best-evidenced model, Maya 2 Native, is locked to CUDA, so the pipeline should be built to swap voice engines cheaply. An MLX port of Maya 2 would make it the immediate front-runner.

The second shift is in what "QA" means. Pronunciation errors that the owner currently catches by ear (aspiration, clipped onsets, Sanskrit conjuncts) can be measured directly with alignment posteriors from MIT-licensed Indic models. Once that measurement exists, every later decision, from voice engine to Sanskrit voice to emotion tags, becomes an evidence-based A/B test that fits the project's no-fabrication rule. Building it first is what makes the rest of the plan trustworthy.
