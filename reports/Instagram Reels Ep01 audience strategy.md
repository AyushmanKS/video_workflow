# Stagger four native Reels, one per evening

Post Episode 01 as **four separate Reels, one language per day, each in an evening IST slot**. Start with Hindi, the largest audience, then Gujarati, Telugu and English. Write each caption originally in its own script, with a hook of 55 characters or less, a single romanized keyword line, at most **5 caption hashtags**, and one "send this to…" call to action. Every container should carry a custom `cover_url`, a deliberate `audio_name`, `share_to_feed=true` and `is_ai_generated=true`. Leave out trial Reels, location and collaborators for now. The reason is that Instagram ranks Reels for non-followers mainly on watch time, likes per reach and **sends per reach**. Caption text and hashtags help Instagram classify a Reel and match it in Search, but they do not raise reach on their own. Posting time matters less than Indian vendor blogs suggest. What a zero-follower account controls is a clean, original upload, a caption that matches what people search for, and a reason to forward the Reel. The audiences differ a lot in size. Hindi has **52.8 crore** first-language speakers, Telugu 8.1 crore and Gujarati 5.5 crore (Census 2011). Gujarati and Telugu also have strong diasporas in the UK, East Africa and the US. Two risks remain open. Instagram has never said how it treats one creator posting four dubbed versions with identical visuals. And the hashtag post counts below are third-party tool estimates. Labels used here: **[OFFICIAL]** means Instagram, Meta or Adam Mosseri, directly or as reported by the press. **[INDUSTRY]** means vendors and blogs. **[INFERENCE]** means reasoning that no source states outright.

## Four audiences of very different sizes and time zones

**[OFFICIAL/INDUSTRY]** India is Instagram's largest market. Its ad audience was **481 million in late 2025, up 22.9% in a year**, and it skews heavily male: **69.7% male, 29.9% female** among adults. DataReportal warns that these are ad-reach estimates, not user counts ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-india)). Comscore's panel puts Instagram at 337 million unique visitors and says Reels make up **69% of all actions** on the app ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/digital/five-years-of-reels-how-instagrams-algorithm-became-indias-tastemaker-10464781)). The format suits the audience. A music-label executive says people in their 20s and 30s now take in devotional content as short clips, while the 40-plus audience listens to full-length content ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/cover/2025/03/15/tips-industries-managing-director-kumar-taurani-interview.html)). That is an interview claim, not a measurement. Instagram publishes no split of users by language, so language audiences have to be estimated from Census speaker counts: **Hindi 528 million, Telugu 81 million, Gujarati 55 million** first-language speakers in 2011, the latest language data available ([Business Standard/PTI](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/10k-more-sanskrit-speakers-in-india-in-2011-census-118071500152_1.html)).

Abroad, the picture is reversed. Gujarati is the main language of about **189,000 people in England and Wales** ([ICAEW on ONS 2021](https://icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2022/dec-2022/Chart-of-the-week-Language-proficiency-in-England-and-Wales)). Canada counts **209,000 Gujarati and 761,000 Hindi** speakers ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-627-m/11-627-m2025004-eng.htm)). In the US, Telugu was the **fastest-growing foreign language, up 86%** between 2010 and 2017 ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/news/telugu-fastest-growing-language-us-says-study-90332)). US totals for 2024 conflict between sources and were not verified against Census tables. **[INFERENCE]** That gives four distinct readers. The **Hindi** viewer is a young man in north or central India, or a worker in the Gulf, 1.5 hours behind IST. The **Gujarati** viewer is in Gujarat or Mumbai, or in the UK, East Africa or the New Jersey and Texas diaspora. The **Telugu** viewer is in Andhra Pradesh or Telangana, or part of a young, tech-heavy US diaspora 10.5 to 13.5 hours behind IST. The **English** viewer is urban pan-Indian or a second-generation diaspora viewer who may not read Indic scripts. The default viewer is a man aged 18 to 34, not a family elder, so the tone should be calm and story-led, not preachy.

One platform fact affects each language differently. Meta AI Reel translation with lip sync now covers **Hindi, English and Telugu** (among others), but **not Gujarati** ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/11/instagram-empowers-creators-to-go-global-with-local-voice-translations-and-fonts/)). This corrects the earlier profile report, which listed only EN↔HI. **[INFERENCE]** Keep translation **off**. The account already ships human-reviewed native versions, so a machine dub would compete with them and add a second AI layer. The Gujarati Reel has no translation fallback at all, so its discovery depends entirely on its own script and tags.

## Captions are search documents; sends are the reach lever

**[OFFICIAL]** Instagram's Search explainer calls the typed query "by far the most important signal". It matches the query against usernames, bios, **captions, hashtags** and places, and it tells creators to "put keywords and hashtags in the caption, not the comments" ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works/)). Since **19 December 2025, posts are capped at 5 hashtags**. Instagram says "fewer (up to 5) more targeted hashtags" work better than generic ones, and it names #reels and #explore as spam tags ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-hashtags-five-per-post-10923075); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/no-more-hashtag-stuffing-instagram-limits-hashtag-usage-to-just-5-per-reel-and-post-507444-2025-12-19)). Mosseri says hashtags "don't significantly increase your reach" ([Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/instagram-search-seo/)). What does drive reach is engagement: he calls **sends per reach** "one of the most important signals we use in ranking" ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389)), and sends count slightly more for non-followers ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)). The caption therefore has two jobs. It must match searches, and it must give the viewer a reason to forward the Reel.

**[INDUSTRY]** Feed captions are cut at about **125 characters**, and the Reels overlay shows roughly 55 to 60 ([Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/insights/instagram-character-limit?hsLang=en); [ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/instagram-reels-captions.html)). A 4,408-post vendor study found that caption length "barely matters" for Reels engagement ([PostPlanify](https://postplanify.com/blog/instagram-caption-length)). **[INFERENCE]** That leads to a fixed structure. **Line 1** is a hook of 55 characters or less that includes the primary keyword. **Lines 2 to 4** tell the story in plain words that people actually search for: Valmiki, Tamasa, krauncha, "Ma nishada", Brahma, first shloka. Next comes the shloka citation, which naturally invites saves, then one romanized keyword line, one send CTA and one save CTA, and finally the hashtags. Use at most two emojis.

The choice of script is an inference, but a well-grounded one. Instagram supports native-script captions and tags and is investing in them: in 2026 Edits added Devanagari fonts and Hindi bilingual auto-captions ([PRmoment India](https://www.prmoment.in/pr-news/instagrams-multilingual-push-is-a-quiet-but-significant-shift-for-indias-communicators)). However, nothing official says that Search cross-matches रामायण with "ramayan". **[INFERENCE]** Write the body in native script so it is authentic and matches native-script queries, and add **one romanized line** so it also matches the much more common romanized searches. Do not add a full English translation, because that pushes the native hook out of view and makes the caption look machine-made. Both spellings, #ramayan and #ramayana, exist as separate tags ([displaypurposes #ramayan](https://displaypurposes.com/hashtags/hashtag/ramayan); [displaypurposes #ramayana](https://displaypurposes.com/hashtags/hashtag/ramayana)). #ramayana is noisy and co-occurs with meme tags such as #tmkoc and #jethalal, so always pair it with #valmiki to anchor the topic ([displaypurposes](https://displaypurposes.com/hashtags/hashtag/ramayana)). Very large tags are crowded: #jaishreeram has about **14.9M posts** by tool estimate ([iqhashtags](https://iqhashtags.com/hashtags/hashtag/jaishreeram)), against roughly 6,500 for #ramayanam ([displaypurposes](https://displaypurposes.com/hashtags/hashtag/ramayana)). **[INFERENCE]** A new account is more likely to stay visible on mid-size, specific tags.

Two tone guardrails apply to a devotional series. Engagement bait ("Comment Jai Shri Ram if…") is a recommendation-guideline risk, so use genuine questions instead. Leave politicised tags such as #hindutva, which tools list as "related", off a storytelling series. India's BNS s.299, which replaced IPC 295A, punishes deliberate, malicious insult to religion, and a single FIR can trigger proceedings ([Drishti IAS](https://law.drishtiias.com/current-affairs/social-media-posts-and-communal-harmony); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/judiciary/iftar-on-ganga-to-mf-husain-paintings-the-elasticity-of-hurting-religious-sentiments-in-indian-courts/2935081/)). Honorifics, "according to Valmiki's Ramayana", the source citation, and avoiding "myth/fake" or "shocking secret" framing all cost nothing and reduce that risk. The story itself, in which a hunter kills the male krauncha by the Tamasa, Valmiki's grief becomes the first verse, and Brahma commissions the epic, matches published retellings ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/pageturner/excerpt/narada-used-sanskrit-grammar-turn-valmikis-curse-into-blessing/2282184)).

The current Ep01 Instagram captions in `content/C0001/gu/publish.json` and `te/publish.json` are only three lines long. They contain no story keywords, no romanized line and no CTA. Their tag sets (#શ્રીરામ / #శ్రీరాముడు, #jaishriram) also leave out #valmiki and #ramayana. The drafts below replace them. The Telugu, Gujarati and Hindi drafts reuse the hooks already approved at G1, but each still needs a native-speaker read before G3.

## Evening IST slots, one language a day

**[INDUSTRY]** Indian posting-time studies are vendor blogs, and they disagree. SocialPilot's analysis of 250,000 Reels gives scattered slots ranging from 6 AM to 10:30 PM ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/insights/best-time-to-post-reels-on-instagram)). Most Indian guides cluster around **6 to 9 PM IST on weekdays**, but none publishes a method ([WsCube Tech](https://www.wscubetech.com/blog/instagram-post-time/); [timetopost.co](https://timetopost.co/best-time-to-post/instagram/india/)). **[OFFICIAL, secondary]** Mosseri says timing "doesn't matter much" and that posting just before followers are active helps only slightly ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/ca/blog/time-post-instagram)). **[INFERENCE]** Treat slots as a modest, testable factor. With no followers, almost all early reach goes to non-followers, so retention and sends outweigh the clock. Once the account has 100 or more followers, switch to the "Most active times" panel in Insights. US daylight saving ended on 1 November 2026 and the UK changed on 25 October, so the offsets below are standard time.

Spacing matters more than the exact slot. **[OFFICIAL]** Instagram matches duplicates on "audio and visual signals" and recommends only the original. It explicitly says new voiceovers are a substantial change that is not swapped out ([Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality)). **[INFERENCE]** Four Reels published within minutes of each other would split a zero-follower account's first-hour signals four ways, fill the grid with one topic, and give a duplicate classifier four near-identical items at once. Posting one language per day gives each Reel its own test audience and its own readable result in Insights, at the cost of spreading Ep01 across four days. If the owner wants to move faster, the fallback is two languages a day, at least 10 hours apart: Telugu at 07:30 IST, which is the US evening, paired with Hindi at 19:30 IST. The order runs from the largest audience to the smallest. Hindi opens because it has the biggest pool to test on. English goes last because its urban Indian viewers have already had three days of the series in their own languages.

## Six API fields to set, four to leave alone

**[OFFICIAL]** For `media_type=REELS` on v27.0, the settable fields are `caption`, `cover_url`, `thumb_offset`, `share_to_feed`, `audio_name`, `is_ai_generated`, `trial_params`, `collaborators` (maximum 3), `location_id` and `user_tags`. The caption limit is 2,200 characters ([IG User Media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media)). `alt_text` is "for image posts only", and Reels are not supported. All the Reel fields above work with both Instagram Login and Facebook Login. Only partnership labels, resumable upload and the 2026 Audio API need Facebook Login ([Content Publishing guide](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing)). The repo's `instagram.py` currently sends only `caption`, `share_to_feed` and `cover_url`/`thumb_offset`, and Ep01 is set to `thumb_offset_ms: 1500`.

**`cover_url`** should be a **1080×1920 sRGB JPEG under 8 MB**, and it overrides `thumb_offset`. The profile grid now crops covers to a **centred 3:4** frame ([Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-updates-jan-2025); [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/instagrams-new-grid-layout-size-and-dimensions-2025/)), so keep the native-script title inside the central 1080×1440 area. Convert each language's existing `thumbnail.png` to JPEG and use it, matching its words to caption line 1. **`audio_name`** can be renamed only once, so set it at creation. A named sound page makes reuse trace back to the account. **`is_ai_generated=true`**: Instagram's help page lists "a reel narrated with a realistic AI-generated voiceover" as needing a label, while cartoon-style visuals do not ([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/761121959519495)). The 2026-06-22 changelog confirms the flag applies the "AI info" label at publish time ([Changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/changelog)). No source shows the label reducing reach. **[INFERENCE]** The chalk visuals alone would not trigger the requirement, but the Malini and Veena TTS narrators do. Note that the YouTube block in the same `publish.json` files says `"synthetic": false`, so the two platforms currently disagree. The owner should decide whether YouTube's disclosure should match. **`share_to_feed=true`** puts the Reel on the grid as well as in the Reels tab. Eligibility for the Reels tab still applies either way.

Leave **`trial_params`** out for Ep01. Trial Reels go only to non-followers until they graduate. Eligibility is disputed, with sources citing 200 or 1,000 followers ([superprofile.bio](https://superprofile.bio/guides/instagram-trial-reels); [AdaptlyPost](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels)), and a zero-follower account has no followers to protect. Revisit trials once the account passes the threshold. At that point the secondary-language versions could run as `SS_PERFORMANCE` trials so they don't stack in followers' feeds. Leave out **`location_id`**, which must be a Facebook Page with location data and has no published reach effect. Leave out **`collaborators`**: the reported advice is that the larger account should start the collab ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-collab-post/)), and there is no partner account. Leave out **`user_tags`**, which has no tagging use here. After publishing, use the app to pin an owner comment teasing Ep02 and asking a real question, reply to early comments, and share the Reel to Stories, which Mosseri reportedly says "will help a bit with reach" ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-collab-post/)).

## YouTube first is safe; self-duplication is the open risk

**[OFFICIAL]** Instagram's originality rules target accounts that post content "without creating it or substantially changing it". Recommended content must show **no visible watermark** ([Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality)). Aggregators reposting other people's work 10 or more times in 30 days stop being recommended ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html)). In April 2026 the rules expanded to photos and carousels, and non-followers stop seeing aggregator accounts while follower feeds stay unchanged ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/05/01/instagram-tweaks-algorithm-to-reduce-reach-of-content-aggregators/)). Agencies read this the same way: a video you made counts as original even if it ran elsewhere first, but third-party watermarks must go ([Vamp](https://vamp.com/insights/how-brands-can-create-more-original-content-to-beat-instagrams-new-algorithm-update/)). **[INFERENCE]** Putting Ep01 on YouTube Shorts first does not make the Instagram copy unoriginal, provided Instagram receives the **clean master render** (`content/C0001/<lang>/C0001-<lang>.mp4`), never a re-downloaded Short and never with a YouTube logo or "Subscribe" end card. No official text names cross-posting your own work from YouTube, so this reading rests on the general rule.

The real uncertainty is the account's own four versions. No official source covers one creator posting dubbed copies with identical visuals. Two facts favour the account. Duplicate matching uses audio as well as visuals, and "new voiceovers" are named as material change. All four versions are also the account's own originals, so the aggregator rule does not obviously apply. Against that, the chalk frames are identical, and a classifier could still choose one version to recommend and quietly suppress the others. Meta's own answer to multilingual reach is the opposite of four posts: **one Reel plus AI translation** ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/11/instagram-empowers-creators-to-go-global-with-local-voice-translations-and-fonts/)). That is a fair argument against this plan. It fails here only because Gujarati is unsupported and because the native narration, script cards and captions are the product. The mitigations are already built into the plan: daily spacing, a distinct native-script cover and on-screen cards per language, captions written rather than translated, and a weekly **Account Status** check. The warning sign of cannibalisation is one version drawing normal non-follower reach while the others stall near zero. If that pattern repeats over the first four or five episodes, move the stalled languages to their own handles. Regional mythology pages already work that way.

## Conclusion

Ep01 is less a launch than a controlled experiment that this plan sets up deliberately. Each language gets its own day and slot, and every container uses the same metadata. Any difference in non-follower reach, sends and watch time between the four versions will then come from language and audience, not from posting timing or fields. That is the evidence this research could not find anywhere public: how Ramayana Reels perform in Gujarati versus Telugu, whether Telugu's US diaspora responds to a morning IST post, and whether Instagram tolerates four dubs of one visual. Read Insights after the four posts, change one variable for Ep02 (the Telugu morning slot is the obvious candidate), and replace these defaults with the account's own data.

Several items stay unverified and should be checked rather than assumed. These are the native-script hashtag volumes, whether the 5-tag cap also counts tags in comments, the Trial Reels follower threshold, and whether Search transliterates between scripts. The policy picture also changed while the earlier profile report was being written. Telugu now has Meta AI translation, and `is_ai_generated` is confirmed for both login types. Recheck the platform rules before each new batch of episodes rather than treating this report as settled.
